About Me

My research focuses on the intersection of computer graphics, differentiable physics, elastoplastic simulation, and 3D Gaussian Splatting. I am particularly interested in how explicit Gaussian representations can be coupled with physically meaningful deformation models while preserving rendering quality, editability, and stable optimization gradients.

My current work studies permanent shape authoring and deformable Gaussian representations, with an emphasis on continuous elastic-to-plastic transition, irreversible plastic memory, and adaptive local refinement driven by physical deformation rather than only image-space reconstruction error.

Graduate Thesis Project

SoftGS

Differentiable Elastoplastic Simulation with Physics-Aware Local Gaussian Refinement

Ongoing

SoftGS is my current thesis project on physically based deformable Gaussian splatting. The system combines the D-EP elastoplastic model with a mesh-driven local Gaussian subdivision strategy. The goal is to support visually clear and physically meaningful permanent deformation while allocating additional geometric and Gaussian resolution only to regions that require it.

The project treats simulation and rendering as a coupled representation: local physical deformation updates the bound Gaussians, while local mesh refinement triggers corresponding Gaussian splitting and rebinding so that geometry, physics, and rendering remain aligned.

Method Overview

D-EP

A differentiable elastoplastic formulation with a bounded smooth plastic activation transition, an exact sub-yield elastic region, and irreversible plastic state storage.

Local Subdivision

Regions with high stress, plastic strain, contact response, or deformation error can be locally refined rather than increasing simulation resolution globally.

Gaussian Refinement

Gaussians associated with refined mesh regions are split and inherit visual and physical attributes, then are rebound to the refined local geometry.

Physical Representation

The simulation follows a finite-deformation elastoplastic decomposition in which the local deformation gradient is separated into elastic and plastic components:

F = Fe Fp

The deformation state is propagated to the Gaussian representation through local mesh–Gaussian binding. After local subdivision, newly created Gaussians inherit appearance and state information from their parent region and are rebound using the refined local geometry.
